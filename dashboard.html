Agricultural Dashboards

Rainfall Analysis

This map shows the average rainfall across the country, dropping from 650mm in the north to under 250mm in the southwest. It visually explains why the north can grow maize, while the dry south and west must rely on tough crops like sorghum and millet.

Temperature Trends

This table directly links rainfall to food production. In 1980/81, there was good rain (603mm) and high production (48,400 tons). In 1982/83, rainfall dropped to 308mm, and production crashed by 80% to 8,500 tons. It shows exactly how vulnerable crops are to low rain.

Crop Yield

This map shows the severity of drought on crop growth across the country. The table next to it shows how much money the government had to spend on drought relief. It proves that bad rainfall is not just a farming problem, but a national economic crisis.

Rainfall vs Production

This bar chart shows how farmers react to unpredictable weather. Instead of just accepting a late harvest, farmers actively "change on crop" (the highest bar). This means they switch to faster-growing or more drought-resistant crops when the rains are delayed.

Soil & Water Management

These graphs show decades of rainfall data. They prove that Botswana has a very short rainy season (mostly January and February) and that the total amount of rain changes drastically every year, causing cycles of floods and droughts.

Rainfall vs Production

This map shows that Botswana has very different soil types depending on the region. The west is mostly sandy soil that loses water quickly, while the north has clay soil that holds water well. This is why most large-scale farming happens in the north and east.
