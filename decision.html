🌍 Farming Decision Guide

📊 Rainfall Classification

Rainfall levels in Botswana vary widely across districts. Farmers can classify their season based on annual rainfall totals:

  • Low Rainfall: Less than 400 mm per year (common in Kgalagadi, Ghanzi).
  • Average Rainfall: 400–800 mm per year (Central, Southern, Kgatleng).
  • High Rainfall: More than 800 mm per year (Chobe, North East, Okavango).

Understanding rainfall counts helps farmers decide which crops and water management strategies to adopt.

🌵 Low Rainfall (Dry Seasons)

Scarce rainfall requires drought-tolerant crops and water conservation. Soil moisture retention is critical.

  • Crops: Sorghum, millet, cowpea, chickpea, pigeon pea, cassava, sweet potato, groundnut, mung bean, amaranth, quinoa, prickly pear cactus, date palm, baobab.
  • Practices: Mulching, drip irrigation, rainwater harvesting.
  • Risks: Drought stress, leaf curling, stunted growth.

🌱 Normal Rainfall (Average Seasons)

Moderate rainfall allows standard planting routines. Balanced soil fertility and crop rotation ensure sustainability.

  • Crops: Maize, sunflower, groundnuts, sweetreed, watermelon, vegetables.
  • Practices: Crop rotation, compost application, moderate irrigation.
  • Risks: Pest outbreaks, nutrient deficiencies.

🌧️ High Rainfall (Wet Seasons)

Excess rainfall can cause waterlogging, erosion, and crop diseases. Drainage and disease monitoring are essential.

  • Crops: Rice (flood-resistant), cassava, maize (wet-tolerant), yam, millet, groundnut.
  • Practices: Raised beds, contour farming, drainage channels.
  • Risks: Root rot, fungal infections, nutrient leaching.

🧪 Soil Identification Guide

Farmers can identify soil type using simple field tests:

  • Sandy Soil: Feels gritty, drains quickly, crops dry out in 4–5 days without rain.
  • Clay Soil: Feels sticky when wet, holds water, poor aeration slows root growth.
  • Loamy Soil: Balanced texture, holds water but drains well, ideal for most crops.

Knowing your soil type helps match crops to rainfall conditions and guides water management strategies.
